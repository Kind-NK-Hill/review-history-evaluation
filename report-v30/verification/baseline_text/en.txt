Abstract
ProbabilityTheoryFormalization uses AI agents and Lean to turn probability-textbook statements and ar-
guments into a maintained collection of formal proofs. Its workflow connects source preparation and
dependency planning with proof implementation, library search, assistance, independent review, repair
and controlled acceptance. This report evaluates what those activities deliver and where their connections
fail, using eleven task groups, detailed execution histories, a reconstruction of historical review records
and checks of saved code.
The three tested organizations are A, an author working through the project’s existing control and review
procedures; B, a lead author choosing how to implement, delegate, check and submit; and C, a separate
coordinator assigning proof work and checks. Under the corrected acceptance criteria, A completes 9/11
groups, B 10/11 and C 11/11. A/B omit the task-permitted eventual-integrability formulation of Scheffé’s
lemma; original A omits the continuous-mapping branch of the vector-convergence group. B/C deliver
complete, disclosed repairs to insufficient continuous-mapping assumptions. Across nine groups with
complete comparable time records, median solver spans are 58.9, 33.2 and 28.7 minutes. These are results
of the executed configurations, including their different models, development versions and host interven-
tions. [12-14,22,23]
The cases explain more than the ordering. In the innovation-to-martingale task, A produces a buildable
proof and several passing opinions, but cannot use those opinions until host recovery; an early C run
submits while disclosing that its requested checks have not arrived. The fourth-moment strong-law proof
improves by deriving a required bound internally. The inversion group connects helper-produced analysis
to later theorem calls, whereas maximal-coupling assistance produces code without an established adoption
chain. Reassessment also corrects the evaluator: it both reverses mistaken rejections and withdraws an
overly broad acceptance. [17-19,22,23,25,27]
Historical analysis retains positive recorded-label change in 128 comparisons with identified candidates
and affirmative same-target decisions, while showing why lower acceptance on later revisions does not es-
tablish diminishing returns. Saved-code checks distinguish compilation, removal of a direct proof shortcut
and agreement with a stated definition. Together, the findings establish substantial mathematical delivery
and specific repair mechanisms. The maintenance priorities are completing the public task interface, get-
1
ting produced work to the next decision maker, and applying the intended mathematical criterion consis-
tently. [8-11]

1 How the project organizes and maintains proofs
1.1 From textbook material to a usable proof library
ProbabilityTheoryFormalization, formerly ToyApollo, develops AI-assisted formalization of a probability
textbook. Its practical goal is to turn the textbook’s definitions and arguments into formal results that
express the intended mathematics and can be used by later chapters. Lean checks the formal declarations
and proofs; Mathlib is the general mathematics library available to those proofs. [26,34]
The workflow begins before an author receives a proof task. The operator supplies the source material,
which is organized into source units such as a numbered subsection or a Problems section. A task plan
identifies the work and its declared dependencies, including explicit references to earlier textbook results.
Additional support for a problem can be selected during implementation. The task package brings together
the source, plan, permitted dependencies, their selection reasons and the working draft. Thus an earlier
theorem is not just a file the author might find: the plan records when the task is intended to rely on it.
[34,35]
The software separates orchestration, proof checking and persistent state. Python coordinates task prepara-
tion, checks and the next eligible actions. AI roles choose and carry out mathematical work. Lean checks
the resulting code. SQLite indexes tasks and retained build, review and application evidence so that work
can be resumed and maintained. Drafts and temporary artifacts are staged separately from the canonical
corpus, the project’s official Lean task files. Acceptance is the step that moves reviewed work into that
corpus. [34]
In the task groups studied here, an agent receives the textbook claim and argument, a task sheet, the permit-
ted library and designated upstream material—existing results the task may use. It must deliver public
declarations, their proof bodies, necessary support—auxiliary mathematical code—and an explanation of
how the task is met. A source obligation is work required by the task; a public premise is an assumption a
caller must supply to use an exported theorem. Making a required intermediate conclusion a public premise
leaves that work to the caller. A candidate is a code version, and its delivery includes the accompanying
submission material. Section 1.4 illustrates these distinctions. [12]
In the execution accounts, author, helper and reviewer denote AI roles, not the human authors of this
report. An author searches and implements; a helper supplies code or analysis; a reviewer inspects the
requested mathematics. A coordinator assigns work and decides what to do with returned results. The
host is the surrounding runtime or operator that dispatches roles, transfers artifacts and can intervene.
The project documentation supplies the operating design described here; the frozen configurations and
execution histories below establish the behavior of the studied runs.

1.3 From a working draft to an accepted task

with Lean, requests independent semantic review, and then repairs or applies the result. Semantic review
checks how source claims and essential proof steps appear in the declarations, assumptions and proof
bodies; it also examines listed downstream callers—the code that uses the result. Its purpose is to assess
the mathematical delivery rather than repeat the compiler’s judgment. [34,35]

A failed review does not always call for another local edit. A missing lemma within an accepted route stays
in ordinary repair. A source mismatch, a missing core argument hidden in a premise, or an unclear proof
route instead pauses ordinary repair for read-only diagnosis. The diagnoser identifies the route or statement
problem; it does not write the proof or replace the semantic reviewer. The repair loop also refuses to send
an unchanged candidate back after a semantic failure. The risk-triggered pre-authoring route check, the
Math Gate, is explained in Section 1.5. [34]
Preparing a service and executing it remain different operations. review‐now prepares the request and
exposes the next action; an outer agent or operator still invokes the reviewer. Similarly, a helper may
produce code in an isolated workspace before the result is exported, read by the lead role, applied and
checked. These handoffs explain why the cases follow what the decision maker actually received, rather
than equating a completed invocation with a usable result. [19,34]
Before applying a passing review, the program checks that it still concerns the candidate, support, source
and review requirements being used. This is the review binding. Only an eligible result can update the
canonical task file; a failed application restores its prior contents. A failed review of an existing official
result normally preserves the code and records the need for repair. This separates exploring a replacement
from accepting it, while retaining the evidence that explains the decision. [34]
1.4 What it means to complete a proof
The historical inversion task thm_9_5, which connects interval probabilities to integrals of a characteristic
function, shows why a valid conditional proof can still leave work unfinished. Its early candidate already
contained substitution, pointwise cases, dominated convergence and rescaling. An assumption called h_
spine supplied three analytical ingredients: integrability, justification for exchanging integrals and the
Dirichlet-integral limit. Successive candidates moved those duties into the proof. [1]

1.5 Route checks, task scheduling and whole-proof responsibility
A parent task owns the original textbook claim. The Math Gate is a risk-triggered check of a natural-
language proof route and proposed Lean statement. It is used for source mismatch, shifting core proof
work into public assumptions or repeated failures without a clear route; most small tasks do not use it. A
go permits ordinary parent-task writing. A stop blocks that activity under the current plan. Historical stops
could still permit designated support work: useful lemmas accumulated during the moment-generating-
function case. A later vector-convergence run received a broader host instruction prohibiting all editing,
assistance and new review. Those were different restrictions imposed by different actors. [6,22,26,34]
Historically, some proof obligations, labeled OBL, became subtasks with their own candidates, reviews
and propagation of completion status. Later rules absorbed them into parent or support files and required
renewed assessment of the assembled parent. Decomposition still produces useful mathematics, but its
pieces must be connected to the final public theorem and its callers. Sections 5 and 6 examine that connec-
tion. [5]
For multiple tasks, batch planning reads the recorded state and dependencies to distinguish work ready for
authoring, work needing review or route checking, and work blocked by an upstream task. It can organize
a worker queue around downstream dependencies and potential work conflicts. The plan identifies the next
actions; the outer agent dispatches the corresponding roles, and each task still follows its own acceptance
path. An empty ordinary-author queue can therefore mean that a reviewer or diagnoser must act next,
rather than that all work is finished or the user must intervene. [34]
The division of responsibility developed from an earlier pipeline with prescribed planning, retrieval and
candidate-selection stages, already including reranking, error feedback and experience injection. Later
package-based editing gave the author more discretion while retaining explicit acceptance controls. Ap-
pendix L.1 traces that development; it is separate from the A/B/C comparison. [3]

1.6 Library interfaces and maintenance after acceptance
The same textbook concept can have different Lean representations in the project and in Mathlib. Importing
a theorem expressed with the project’s expectation, for example, does not by itself convert it into a theorem
about a Mathlib integral. For important shared concepts, the documented policy is to introduce the textbook
definition and basic properties, prove a translation theorem connecting that interface to Mathlib, and then
usually use the Mathlib form in later work. This is a policy for recurring mathematical interfaces, not a
required cycle for every local lemma. Existing runnable files are kept unless a concrete reuse problem
calls for an interface repair. [35]
Dependency selection follows actual use. Mentioning expectation in a source passage supplies mathemat-
ical context; it does not automatically require importing the project’s expectation definition. An import is
justified by the declaration, earlier theorem or translation the proof needs, and the dependency trail records
that reason. General library infrastructure is legitimate support. If an auxiliary theorem carries the assigned
task’s mathematical content, however, review must inspect it as part of that task’s proof, not exempt it be-
cause it is named a bridge. The variance-expansion comparison in Section 4.1 and the Gamma/Dirichlet
history in Section 6.1 examine this distinction in concrete proofs. [35]
Acceptance does not end maintenance. A changed definition or theorem signature can require callers to
provide different arguments or assumptions. The task and dependency records identify affected work;
builds and review check the revised interfaces and their use. SQLite keeps a rebuildable index of the
underlying evidence, separating an earlier passing review, coverage of the current code and support, and
a working candidate that still needs attention. A change requires the relevant coverage to be checked
again, not merely retention of a pass label. The total-variation repair in Section 6.2 shows this obligation
concretely: the source’s probability-measure condition had to reach both the definition and its callers.
[7,34]

2.2 How post-submission evaluation produced the current decisions
Common evaluation is the study’s assessment after submission, separate from help and internal review
during solving and from production acceptance. The original endpoint procedure requested two fresh, read-
only semantic opinions on the source, task, candidate, permitted upstream material and recorded technical
checks. Configuration metadata was withheld from the evaluator-facing request. Matching pass or fail
votes completed the decision; two inconclusive votes left it unresolved. Different votes triggered at most
one fresh adjudicator, who read the same materials and the two opinions and resolved their substantive
disagreement rather than choosing by confidence. All original, consistency and targeted endpoint calls use
the recorded Sol medium configuration. 30; [Appendix N.6]
A group decision concerns the required complete delivery. Passing the coordinate-convergence theorem
alone does not complete the two-target vector-convergence group, and a verdict on explicitly partial ma-
terial describes that material rather than the missing task. Invalid result formatting is also separated from
mathematics. In the representation-theorem case, a reviewer wrote a pass but used an object where confi-
dence had to be a number; the host recorded an inconclusive execution result and the procedure proceeded
to adjudication. [22,28,30]
Reassessment then examined inconsistent interpretations in variance expansion, Scheffé’s lemma and
Cauchy scope. Each candidate received separate initial opinions under the stated readings. A necessary ad-
judication could be triggered by disagreement on an obligation, an invalid result, or inconsistent treatment
of corresponding behavior across candidates. These adjudicators could see the new initial opinions and a
cross-candidate consistency summary; they were not blind to that later evidence. For the inversion group,
the original submission explanations were supplied symmetrically after the six initial reviews, when the
anonymous code-only view proved insufficient for a documentation judgment. [23,30]
The final acceptance adopted four explicit readings: permitted general expansion for variance additiv-
ity, the task’s eventual-tail formulation for Scheffé, the textbook’s standard-Cauchy core, and reasonable
disclosed repairs of the deficient continuous-mapping assumptions. At 09:22 on 12 September, existing
opinions were reused, determinable format mismatches were repaired and outcomes were recomposed
under those criteria. The two additional continuous-mapping opinions had already been obtained in the
05:17 supplement. This was a retrospective criterion correction, not new solver work. Section 4 gives its
mathematical basis and retains the original outcomes. [13,15,22,30]
Appendix N.6 states the stage-specific rules, evaluation inputs and masking limits. Here independent
means the documented separation of reviewer sessions and roles; adjudicators deliberately receive earlier
evidence, and using separate sessions of the same model does not establish independent model errors.

5.7 Cross-case synthesis: the same symptom can require a different explanation
The cases support comparisons of mechanisms, not just a taxonomy of failures. Three comparisons are
particularly informative.
First, review content and the ability to act on it are separate system properties. A-L1 has several
positive opinions but cannot apply them until recovery. B r4-L1 receives actionable interface advice and
changes the declaration. C r3-L1 receives a passing opinion and submits unchanged code. Review count
cannot substitute for delivery, and code-change count cannot substitute for useful checking. The observed
role of feedback depends on its content, availability and the decision it enables. [20,27]
Second, proof decomposition helps only when the corresponding task is reassembled. H1’s analyt-
ical support enters inversion and later theorem calls; B-M1 integrates a complete implementation. M2
demonstrates closure of a source-facing premise gap, while A-L3 demonstrates a local repair followed by
an unresolved broader interface. The existence of useful subproofs and the completeness of the final task
are both substantive observations, not competing interpretations from which only one should be reported.
[17,18,23,25]
Third, control and evidence-validation rules can protect correctness while also creating delivery
dependencies. The H1 binding gate rejects an opinion attached to the wrong input, which is distinct
from rejecting the proof. A-L1’s inability to obtain readable review results blocks progress through its
acceptance path. A-L7’s broader host stop removes otherwise possible next actions. These observations
argue for distinguishing the purpose of a control from faults in executing it; they do not show that removing
all gates would preserve the same evidence or improve outcomes. [17,22,27]
For maintainers, the resulting diagnosis is more specific than “the model made mistakes.” The terminal
omissions in this panel lie in source-obligation coverage. Tool records show pervasive environment fric-
tion. Particular trajectories establish that transport, review-basis handling and host decisions can interrupt
delivery of mathematics already produced. The material does not provide a population-wide ranking of
independent root causes or time lost, but it does identify concrete failure locations and prevents attributing
every long or unsuccessful run to mathematical inability.

6.4 Recover distinct judgments before measuring change
The historical September 1 snapshot contains 3,820 database rows about 453 task objects. Those rows
are not 3,820 independent model judgments. Reconstruction separates direct-evidence signatures, dupli-
cate and object-binding references, collector claims and application pointers. A signature uses the result-
evidence digest and review-input digest; task, candidate, label and context fields are checked for consis-

generated records and representation copies are then treated separately. 10; [Appendix C.1]

viewer, application or normalization failures, and a test. Among 251 representation merges, 223 pair raw
and normalized outputs. Text-identical copies without a production pointer are not automatically merged.
This prevents software failure from becoming a mathematical rejection and prevents one opinion saved
twice from becoming two independent opinions. The resulting branches retain their own units rather than
being presented as one successively shrinking funnel. [10]
Checking only the main file still misses meaningful differences. Among the original 2,645 directed rela-
tions, 98 keep that file unchanged while commonly bound non-main objects differ; thirteen of those rela-
tions change from fail to pass. These are observed changes in the review basis, not hypothetical caveats
about hashes. For thm_11_8, the later upstream source is recovered but the earlier one is not, so the report
establishes changed evidence without inventing the mathematical difference. For thm_9_7, the registered
early candidate differs from the candidate actually judged; the corresponding relation is excluded rather
than silently repaired into a different pair. [9,10]
The reconstructed archive is consequently useful for investigating changes, but a main-file match is not
enough to estimate reviewer inconsistency on fixed evidence. Identity of a record, identity of a whole
review input and semantic comparability of the mathematical target require different checks.

6.5 Label growth remains observable after qualification, but its scope changes
The broad comparison selects 461 directly bound relationships across 248 tasks. Earlier endpoints contain
116 pass labels and later endpoints 324, a net increase of 208. Giving every relationship equal weight
yields a 45.12-percentage-point increase; first averaging within a task and then weighting tasks equally
yields 57.42 points. Neither is the fraction of textbook tasks eventually completed. A task with more
comparisons contributes more to the first quantity, and shared endpoints prevent treating every relationship
as independent. 10; [Appendix C.3]
The analysis also asks what remains after affirmative target comparability and valid object binding are both
required. Within the 462 managed relations, source-aware, unblinded AI archival readings supply 129
affirmative target decisions and two confirmed target boundaries. The other 331 relations lack a target-
specific substantive decision; a screen that finds no change is insufficient for affirmative qualification.
Programmatic binding checks are applied separately. Appendix C.4 explains the decision requirements
and gives examples of all three dispositions. The resulting qualified subset contains 128 comparisons
across 79 tasks, with 28 earlier and 45 later passes, a net increase of seventeen. Its relationship-weighted
increase is 13.28 points and task-weighted increase 20.46 points. This is a positive descriptive result within
an explicitly qualified subset, not merely a statement that the broad data is unusable. [10]

6.6 A diminishing-returns interpretation fails a selection check
The follow-up study first fixes 230 failing origins across 182 tasks. These are the first failing endpoints
of saved connected segments, not every subsequent failure reopened as a new trial. Only afterward does
the analysis require target comparability and valid object binding for each follow-up step. Origins with no
qualifying first step remain in the denominator. 10; [Appendix C.5]
Strict follow-up observes 25 first acceptances before exit. Of 205 exits, 186 lack an affirmative target-
comparability decision, two have a confirmed target or source-convention change, one has an object con-
23
flict and sixteen reach a saved-fragment end. Most exits therefore indicate unestablished comparability,
not demonstrated drift or final mathematical failure.
A tempting pattern appears in the observed steps: 22/69 qualified first steps reach acceptance, versus 3/31
later steps. But later steps are contributed by paths that have not already passed and remain observable.
The saved analysis checks the tasks producing those later steps: only fifteen tasks do so, and their nineteen
first steps contain just one pass. The lower later-step proportion is therefore entangled with which tasks
enter later follow-up. The comparison does not isolate the effect of making another revision. [10]
This selection check is a substantive analytical result. It identifies an appealing but unsupported expla-
nation and demonstrates the changing composition behind it. Broader follow-up reaches more recorded
acceptances from the same origins, but that answers record reachability under looser requirements rather
than restoring missing fixed-target evidence. Appendix C.5 -C.9 gives the origin construction, risk sets,
broader paths and exact denominators.
6.7 Saved-code checks distinguish compilation, proof shortcuts and definition behavior
Fourteen frozen pairs were selected for code checks: six development pairs and eight further pairs chosen
by a seeded, stratified rule after excluding previously exposed tasks. Pair identities were fixed before later
eligibility and execution outcomes. This is not a representative benchmark, but it preserves failed and
unavailable measurements instead of replacing inconvenient pairs. 11; [Appendix D.1]
Of the 28 files, seventeen compile and sixteen yield the required named declaration. All sixteen extracted
targets pass both saved axiom policies; the extra compiling file lacks the requested declaration. Across the
eight further pairs, each side has six compiling files, but def_13_2 changes from failure to success while
prob_10_1 changes the other way. An unchanged aggregate count therefore hides different artifact-level
transitions. The historical runner has no cross-endpoint type-equality gate, so successful extraction on both
sides still does not establish a common mathematical target. [11]
prob_10_1 gives a concrete semantic finding: an early theorem takes precisely its desired conclusion as a
premise and returns it. The later source removes that defect, but the file fails in the saved build environment.
The evidence supports removal of a direct shortcut, not certification of a complete repaired implementation.
These are separate outcomes worth reporting together.
For def_2_1, the source convention calls a set countable when it is in bijection with all natural numbers. The
earlier library predicate also accepts finite sets. The empty set satisfies the latter but cannot have a bijection
with the naturals, since zero would have no preimage. Saved Lean witnesses check the early predicate, the
later predicate and the written reference, including agreement of the later predicate with the reference for
all sets. This makes a disputed interpretation testable as a formal proposition. The interpretation linking
source text to the reference remains an AI-assisted judgment, not independent expert certification; the
witnesses nevertheless establish precise behavior of the formal predicates. 11; [Appendix D.5]
The same study asks whether evaluating both candidates under both rules adds information beyond a fair
fixed-rule comparison. For the inspected axiom and direct-premise attributes, effective rules coincide or
have known implications, and the additional cells provide no extra answers. Seven constructed proof
scenarios show that extra cells can add answers under deliberately arranged conditions; they validate the
comparison program, not additional real-task success. The distinction between those scenarios and the
historical negative result is retained in Appendix D, together with all pair states and question counts. A
more elaborate evaluation table is useful only when its added measurements resolve a question the simpler,
logically informed comparison cannot already answer. [11]

7 Overall assessment
7.1 What the system has demonstrated
The selected configurations complete substantial mathematical work: a fourth-moment strong law, stopped-
expectation arguments, Skorokhod representation and an inversion-to-uniqueness application chain. The
cases locate constructed objects, local derivations, changes to public assumptions and adopted support.
Under current acceptance, C completes all eleven groups, B ten and A nine; A has the highest recorded
median span and role count. These are the study’s comparative results for the executed configurations.
[13,14,17,18,25,28]
The remaining gaps are equally concrete. A/B’s Scheffé results do not expose the permitted eventual-
tail interface. Original A omits continuous mapping, and its supplementary repair still omits composite
measurability. B/C complete disclosed repairs to the insufficient continuous-mapping conditions. Beyond
the common core, B supplies the stricter optional variance self-expansion, while C supplies the wider
Cauchy parameter family and a separately exposed numerical continuous-mapping core. Completion and
additional scope should both remain visible. [21-23]
7.2 What explains the successes and failures
Required mathematics has to reach the public theorem. The fourth-moment case closes a genuine
premise gap by proving the missing bound internally. The Gamma history shows that removing an axiom
can still leave the intended derivation absent. The moment-generating-function and coupon histories locate
further work in global assembly, distributional connection and normalization. These distinctions explain
why substantial progress can precede whole-task acceptance, and why an earlier pass need not contain
more mathematics than a later fail. [4,6,8,25]
Produced work has to reach the actor who can use it. The innovation cases show different submission
behavior when review opinions are unavailable. Stopped-process and inversion runs establish successful
adoption and subsequent use; maximal coupling supplies a counterexample to inferring adoption from a
completed helper. The relevant sequence is production, return, reading, application and validation. Which
link succeeds determines what the feedback or delegation contributes. [17-20,27]
The evaluation must apply the intended obligation consistently. Correcting covariance-expansion treat-
ment, eventual-tail scope, Cauchy extensions and continuous-mapping assumptions changes the original
aggregate ordering without new solver proofs. The correction preserves real omissions as well as recov-
ering valid results. Evaluator inputs and processing are consequently part of the system diagnosis, not
clerical details separate from the reported performance. [13,15,21-23,30]
7.3 What the historical audit adds
The historical analysis converts repeated representations into identifiable judgments, checks whether com-
pared objects and targets correspond, and retains positive recorded-label change in the qualified subset. It
then tests an explanation of later-revision outcomes against the tasks that actually reach those later steps.
The selection check rejects a simple diminishing-returns interpretation without discarding the observed
acceptances. Saved-code checks add precise statements about declaration existence, axiom dependencies,
direct proof shortcuts and definition behavior. [10,11,32]
These studies support local mechanisms and explicitly selected descriptive results. They do not yet supply
an overall valid-feedback adoption rate: the uniform-depth audit covers six of 127 services, while the sep-
arately investigated cases have their own coverage. Shared models, changing implementations, purposive
25
historical qualification and incomplete cost records also prevent treating the results as an isolated coordi-
nation effect or a complete allocation of project effort. The relevant limits are attached to the method and
metric they affect. [16,30-32]
7.4 Maintenance priorities supported by the evidence
The first priority is whole-task interface closure : finish the actual public scope still missing in Scheffé
and continuous mapping, and check that already proved support is connected to the final declarations and
affected callers. The histories show why neither a successful build nor a list of passed auxiliary tasks settles
that question. [5,7,22,23]
The second is reliable delivery through the existing controls . An author needs access to the review or
helper artifact before it can act on it; the repaired result must still be bound to the code being accepted.
The innovation and inversion cases identify failures in this path even when mathematical work has already
succeeded. They support repairing the return and recovery path while retaining the distinction between
proof review and evidence validation. [17,19,27,33]
The third is evaluation input and criterion maintenance . Documentation judgments need the original
delivery explanation, equivalent library behavior needs the same rule, and recoverable formatting errors
need to remain separate from proof defects. The completed reassessment demonstrates both the cost of
getting these distinctions wrong and how existing evidence can correct the account. [15,21-23,30]
The resulting assessment is that this project can organize and complete serious textbook formalization, with
demonstrated local repair and reuse. Its most informative failures occur where a local proof must become
a complete task, where an artifact must become usable by another role, or where a mathematical delivery
must be judged against the intended criterion. Those are concrete maintenance problems identified by the
combined experiment, trajectories and historical analysis—not conclusions obtainable from compilation
or pass counts alone.
