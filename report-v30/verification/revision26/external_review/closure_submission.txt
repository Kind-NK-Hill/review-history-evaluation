### 4.1 明确目标范围，并检查交付是否覆盖它

第一批Scheffé引理任务（L3）说明了范围差别为什么会影响完成结果。原任务说明允许采用最终尾部可积表述，后来明确的共同数学成果标准要求覆盖这一范围。旧A、B交付要求函数序列的每一项都可积；从某一项开始可积的输入则允许前面有限项不可积，不能直接交给旧定理。要覆盖这类输入，作者需要截取可积的尾部，在尾部应用论证，再把极限结论接回原序列。第3.1节的三项改判依据已有交付及同一输入上的通用库事实；旧L3则还缺少尾部输入构造及原序列恢复的论证。[附录R.1.2](appendix.zh-CN.md#r1-2)及[Q.4](appendix.zh-CN.md#q4)

**检查最终交付。**评审者也需要核对上述范围。对两份固定旧代码进行的新评审中，原指令下A的三份意见都通过，B的两份通过；明确要求核查尾部范围后，两份代码各自的三份意见都要求修复。B在原指令下的三份意见其实都讨论了范围问题，其中两份仍把它当作可选推广。识别问题和把问题列为验收要求，是这组记录中的两个不同决定。

### 4.1 Establish the required scope and check the delivered result

**Establishing scope.** The textbook statement, task instructions, and library assumptions must be aligned. The author needs to identify which inputs the task covers, which properties must be derived within the proof, and which generalizations are additional work. These decisions determine the required delivery. [Appendix R.1](appendix.en.md#r1) preserves the mathematical grounds for the individual judgments.

The first-batch Scheffé task (L3) shows why scope changes the completion result. The original task instructions permitted an eventually integrable tail formulation; the subsequently clarified common mathematical-outcome criterion required coverage of that scope. The old A and B deliveries required every function in the sequence to be integrable. An eventually integrable input permits finitely many initial terms to be nonintegrable and therefore cannot be passed directly to the old theorem. Covering these inputs requires selecting an integrable tail, applying the argument there, and transferring the limiting conclusion back to the original sequence. The three reassessments in Section 3.1 rely on existing deliveries and general library facts on the same input; old L3 still lacks the tail-input construction and the transfer argument. [Appendix R.1.2](appendix.en.md#r1-2) and [Q.4](appendix.en.md#q4)

**Checking the final delivery.** Reviewers must examine the same scope. In new reviews of the two fixed old candidates, all three A reviews and two B reviews passed under the original instructions. After an explicit request to check the tail requirement, all three reviews of each candidate required repair. All three original-instruction B reviews had discussed the scope issue, but two treated it as an optional generalization. Recognizing a problem and making it an acceptance requirement were separate decisions in these records.

Two separately conducted targeted repairs subsequently added the tail interfaces and passed common acceptance. The repairs show that these gaps can be resolved under explicit requirements; the fixed-code diagnostic reviews show what reviewers noticed and required. [Appendix R.1.6](appendix.en.md#r1-6) and [Q.4](appendix.en.md#q4) preserve all twelve opinions, the repair instructions, and the code changes. ### 4.3 Deliver work and enable its continuation

**Delivering assistance.** A helper may prove one lemma or implement an entire task group. The author continuing the work needs readable code, integration, and a checked call to the result. In the first-batch inversion task (H1), the helper's result was used in uniqueness and subsequent applications. Other assistance produced code whose use in the final proof was not confirmed. Assessing adoption requires the returned material, code changes, and call sites. [Appendix R.2.3–R.2.5](appendix.en.md#r2-3) preserves these comparisons. [[17-19,28]](#ref-17)

**Resuming execution.** File access, command entry points, result transfer, and external stop instructions can interrupt proof work. The agent must use the actual response to identify a possible next action. First-batch A-L7's final theorem signature and proof body were already present in the frozen working candidate. Once continuation was authorized, the author explicitly disclosed the statement correction and completed review and delivery. The additional window brought an existing proof through acceptance under clarified criteria. [Appendix R.2.6](appendix.en.md#r2-6) gives the states before and after continuation. [[39]](#ref-39)

These problems connect the final result to intermediate work: retrieved material must fit the goal, local proofs must reach their users, and the delivery must cover the required scope. The next section follows those connections in the second-batch records, reporting the common tracking results before examining individual trajectories.

The first support candidates in both tasks were replaced. The retrieval table in Section 5.2 has only one replacement record because A-M3's first retrieval starts from the main author's earlier construction need, whose later work entered the final proof. The usefulness of assistance thus has concrete checks: what the author received and which material entered the subsequent proof. These cases establish connections or breaks at those locations. They do not measure time saved by assistance relative to working alone.
